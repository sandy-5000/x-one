Combinatorial Identities

What it is

Key identities that turn counting problems into one-liners — memorize the common ones.

Essential identities

IdentityFormula
PascalC(n,k) = C(n-1,k-1) + C(n-1,k)
VandermondeC(m+n,r) = Σ C(m,k)C(n,r-k)
Hockey stickΣ C(i,r) = C(n+1,r+1) for i=r..n
Sum of rowΣ C(n,k) = 2^n
Alternating rowΣ (-1)^k C(n,k) = 0
C(n,k) = C(n,n-k)symmetry
Multinomialn!/(a!b!c!) for a+b+c=n

Vandermonde use case

Choose r items from m+n total = choose k from first m and r-k from next n.

Hockey stick

Sum of diagonals in Pascal triangle — common in combinatorics proofs.

When to use

  • Simplify double sums over binomial coefficients.
  • Prove DP transition matches closed form.
  • CF math problems with "choose from two groups."

Examples

1. CF 118D — Caesar's Legions

Split soldiers into groups — Vandermonde-style.

2. CF 455A — Boredom

Counting with binomial coefficients.

3. Paths with restrictions — hockey stick.

4. CF 1103E — Konrad and Company Evaluation

Heavy combinatorics identities.

5. Prove Σ k*C(n,k) = n*2^(n-1) — differentiate (1+x)^n.
